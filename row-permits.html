What the driveway ordinance requires

Ordinance 2024-04 — the specs you'll be held to

Adopted June 3, 2024. These are the numbers the Town Board checks against, so it's worth reading before you order material or hire an excavator.

RequirementStandard
CulvertMinimum 12 in. diameter × 24 ft long, unless the Board specifies otherwise. Both ends riprapped or sodded. Fill must not cover the ends.
Driveway width12 ft roadbed for a single property.
Overhead clearance15 ft, so fire and EMS apparatus can get in and out.
Distance from lot lineAt least 15 ft from the nearest side lot line — unless it's a shared driveway serving two adjacent parcels, which may sit on the line with recorded maintenance and easement agreements.
SlopeNo more than 5% up or down within 50 ft of the road centerline. The Board may allow more where existing slopes exceed 10%, but never above 15%, and anything over 10% needs rounding at the road.
AngleMeet the road at right angles where possible, with adequate stopping sight distance. Not on sharp curves.
Right-of-wayAssumed to extend 33 ft from the centerline of the Town road unless documented otherwise.
RestorationRoad surface, slopes, shoulders, ditches and vegetation disturbed by the work get restored by the property owner. Culvert installation and maintenance is the owner's cost too.
Private roads (3+ homes)66 ft easement, 12 ft minimum graveled width over 4 in. compacted gravel, 50 ft turnaround, bridges rated for a 30-ton fire apparatus at 10 ft minimum width.
Temporary drivewaysRestored to natural state within 6 months after use stops.
Don't buy the culvert yet. Twelve inches by twenty-four feet is the floor, not automatically the answer. The Board can require something larger depending on the ditch grade and drainage. Wait for the permit.
How it works

From application to inspection

Under Ordinance 2024-04 the Town Board approves or denies every driveway permit — this isn't something the Clerk can sign off on alone. Plan around the meeting schedule.

1

Submit to the Town Clerk

A completed application, the fee, and a sketch map showing roughly where the project goes and its dimensions. You can send the sketch to the Board early for initial comments, but a permit won't be granted until the full package is in § VII(B).

2

The Board may ask for more

Plat map, aerial photo, soil or slope analysis, construction plan — the Board can require any of these depending on the site. Getting them in early avoids losing a month.

3

Board decides at a regular meeting

Regular meetings are the second Monday of the month at 6:00 PM, Town Hall, 7228 CTH W. Approve, approve with conditions, or deny. Conditions attached to a permit are binding on you. A denial comes with a written explanation of the facts it was based on § VII(D).

4

Build it

Call Diggers Hotline at 811 at least three working days before you dig — that's state law, separate from the Town permit. Your permit is good for one year, renewable once for six months. After that it's a new application and a new fee § VII(F).

5

Tell the Board you're done

Within 30 days of finishing. The Board then inspects within 30 days of your notice to confirm it matches the permit and the ordinance § VII(E). Don't skip this step — an uninspected driveway is an open permit.

Two denials on the same parcel and a third application can't be considered for 60 days. If the Board turns something down, ask what would fix it before you refile.

Apply for a driveway permit

Submit the completed application and the fee to the Town Clerk. The Board reviews applications at its regular meeting — the second Monday of the month, 6:00 PM, at the Town Hall, 7228 CTH W. Get it in early enough to make the agenda.

1. The application

Fill it out, attach a sketch showing property lines, grade, slope, width, length, turnoffs and erosion control, and stake out the proposed driveway before inspection.

Download the application

Or call the Clerk at 715-686-2123 and one goes out to you.

2. The fee

$100, non-refundable, due with the application. Pay online or by check to the Town of Winchester.
